The path of an idea

Three zones: the data, the trial factory and the evaluation. An idea moves from one to the next, and most stop on the way; only the proof's wire touches the vault.

  1. 1

    The data

    A value only comes in at the moment it was known. Three windows: development for screening, 2022-2023 for the judge, and an encrypted vault from 2024.

  2. 2

    The trial factory

    A queue of specifications, one network-less container per trial, and four registries that can only be appended to.

  3. 3

    The evaluation

    Screening, at most 12 candidates, then the frozen judge, calibrated by its bench of synthetic worlds.

  4. 4

    The proof

    Only one, on the vault, with a written go. It is the only wire that leaves the vault.

The protocol, like a clinical trial

In pharma, thousands of molecules are screened, then an independent trial decides, once. The lab follows the same logic.

The story of the judge

The judge was not written in one go. Each version was born from a bench that contradicted what we believed.

  1. 06/11

    A criterion with no source

    What we believed
    A maximum-loss threshold was among the criteria.
    What the bench showed
    It rested on no source.
    The rule that follows
    Removed: no figure out of thin air.

  2. 09/11

    The judge trains elsewhere

    What we believed
    Its defects were found while judging real batches.
    What the bench showed
    Each defect found that way had already touched a real result.
    The rule that follows
    It now only improves on synthetic worlds; only a frozen version judges real batches.

  3. 17/11

    A step rejected

    What we believed
    Standardising returns by their volatility was meant to make the test fairer.
    What the bench showed
    The bench rejected it, and the check on synthetic data confirmed a real result, not a bug.
    The rule that follows
    It becomes a yes-or-no check, on the relevant families only, listed before any measurement.

  4. 18/11

    Three fixes out of six refuted

    What we believed
    Fixes to the judge, chosen from our reading notes.
    What the bench showed
    The bench refuted three out of six.
    The rule that follows
    A design sheet before any code: exact source and page, validity conditions, a numeric prediction for each world, and what would make it fail.

  5. 21/11

    One tenth too many

    What we believed
    The cross-sectional judge aimed at 7% false positives at most.
    What the bench showed
    The bench measures 71 out of 1,000, i.e. 7.1%.
    The rule that follows
    No exception: a diagnosis with its sheet, then a new bench on fresh seeds.

A page from the notebook

One of the 21 lessons, chosen because the mistake came from our own reading of a paper, and the bench is what caught it.

L002 · 18/11

Effective N in Benjamini-Hochberg

Belief

Replacing the number of tests with an effective number corrects the redundancy between batches.

Actual source

The effective number is a family-wise error tool (Bonferroni, Šidák). Benjamini-Hochberg keeps its false discovery rate under positive dependence with the raw number (Benjamini-Yekutieli, 2001). Our reading note had transposed it.

Bench

With the effective number, the measured false discovery rate rises to about 8 times the ceiling.

Rule

Check which error criterion a method is proven for before plugging it in elsewhere.

What it stands on

    The agents working in the lab go through the same locks: a bench refuses to start without a sheet, a launch is blocked if the registry is not up to date.
