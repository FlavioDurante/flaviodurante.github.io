Forecasting benchmark: foundation models vs a tuned LightGBM

Eight forecasting models, the same data, twelve French regions never used before, and rules written down before the first forecast.

Architecture

Two open sources feed the bench. Every model forecasts the same dates with the same information, then a judge whose rules were written before the first forecast scores them, and a separate script recomputes every number.

  1. 1

    Open data

    Hourly consumption of 12 regions (RTE eCO2mix), and the ECMWF weather forecasts as they were archived at the time.

  2. 2

    The bench

    8 models: LightGBM with and without weather, Chronos-2 with and without temperature, Chronos-Bolt, TimesFM 2.5, MSTL, seasonal naive.

  3. 3

    The judge

    Probabilistic error (WQL), point error (MASE), Diebold-Mariano tests, Holm correction over 21 comparisons fixed in advance.

  4. 4

    The check

    An independent script recomputes every score and every test without the bench's code.

What could bias it

What prevents it

  1. The baseline

    LightGBM

    A weak baseline makes the foundation models look good. In v1, LightGBM had fixed settings and its 80 % intervals covered only 66 %.

    Settings tuned on a validation window and frozen before the test; intervals recalibrated by conformal prediction: coverage from 57-68 % to 75-77 %.

  2. The weather

    ECMWF IFS

    Using the observed temperature as the forecast gives a foresight no operator has.

    Real forecasts as they were archived at the time, issued at least 24 hours before; the same information given to Chronos-2.

  3. The test data

    12 regions

    Fixing the model, then scoring the same period again, is p-hacking.

    Twelve regional series never scored before; protocol committed in git before downloading them.

  4. The comparisons

    DM · Holm

    Out of 21 comparisons, some look significant by chance.

    Diebold-Mariano tests with the HLN correction, then Holm over the 21 pairs fixed in advance.

  5. Leakage

    Timestral

    A model trained on data that overlaps the test period.

    Shown apart, outside the official comparisons, with a slice after its release date.

  6. Failures

    status file

    A crashed run quietly replaced by a better one.

    Reported as failed, left in the comparisons with p = 1, not rerun.

  7. The scoring

    independent script

    A bug in the bench's own scoring code.

    Every score and test recomputed by a script that does not import the bench: no gap beyond one in a million.

The v1 report stays published as it is, with its caveats.

Limits

  • Foundation models used zero-shot, as released: no fine-tuning on these series.
  • One domain, electricity consumption, where temperature is a known driver: the result may not carry over to sales or finance series.
  • About one year of test from November 2025, on 12 regions.
  • Weather reaches Chronos-2 only as temperature, through its covariate input; TimesFM and Chronos-Bolt get none.

Cost, CPU only: LightGBM peaks at 533 MB of memory, the foundation models at 1.5 to 2.2 GB; every model forecasts a window in 0.1 to 2.6 seconds.

How the project ran

  1. 01

    State of the art and v1 protocol

    Papers on time-series foundation models read first; protocol, metrics and reference written before any test forecast (20/11/2026).

  2. 02

    v1: national consumption

    Nine models on French hourly consumption, 384 forecast dates. Timestral seemed ahead, 15 % better at 24 hours.

  3. 03

    Reading v1 critically

    The LightGBM had never been tuned and its intervals were too narrow, no model had weather, and Timestral came out during the test period. v1 stays published as it is.

  4. 04

    v2 pre-registered

    A new protocol committed in git before downloading the regional data and before any tuning (21/11/2026).

  5. 05

    Tuning, weather, calibration

    LightGBM settings frozen on a tuning window, archived ECMWF forecasts for the largest city of each region, conformal calibration.

  6. 06

    Test and check

    12 regions, 3 horizons, 129 forecast dates. One failed run reported as such; every number recomputed independently.
